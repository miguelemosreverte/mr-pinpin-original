# Arrival-10 pose repair and window topology

Pre-production revision, 2026-09-22. Based on direct reinspection of
`arrival-10-v2.png`, `arrival-01.png`, `arrival-02-v2.png` and
`arrival-03-v2.png` after Miguel's feedback.

## Why the earlier anatomy pass was insufficient

The prior audit correctly recognized a more distinct infant torso in 10-v2,
but incorrectly accepted the overall carry. Counting visible arms does not
validate which shoulder they emerge from. Mama's legs/pelvis indicate movement
away while the exposed cream chest, infant and two arm actions imply a much
more frontal upper body. The apparent two arms on one side and implausibly
exposed baby are reasons to reject the pose, even without a literal third arm.
This finding supersedes the earlier arrival-10-v2 pass.

## Chosen staging: let the body occlude the baby

Camera is behind Mama and slightly to her anatomical **left**, looking toward
the closed front door. Mama's pelvis, rib cage and shoulders share a direction
toward that door; her quilled back is the dominant visible surface. Her head
alone turns left toward PinPin, who walks beside her on that side. Keep the
turn modest: one cheek/profile and a connected eyeline, not a frontal face
attached to a backward body. Both legs follow the same direction as the pelvis.

Mama's near **left arm** is free. It emerges from her left shoulder, passes
through one visible elbow and reaches diagonally forward toward the door.
Mama's far **right arm** carries PomPom against her front-right hip. That arm
has a bent elbow and a supporting forearm beneath the baby's bottom, but the
carry is mostly hidden behind her torso from this camera. At most show the
baby's small quilled crown/ear beyond the far contour, plus a small amount of
the far carrying elbow if the perspective allows it. Do not force visibility
of his face, cream belly, feet or the hidden hand. Do not draw a second arm
across the visible near side to make the baby readable.

The previous and next scenes establish that she carries PomPom. This shot's
job is the walk to the door. A coherent occluded carry is better than rotating
only the infant and chest toward the camera. If a future variation requires
the whole baby visible, rotate Mama's pelvis, rib cage, shoulders and foot
placement together into a different three-quarter side pose before rendering;
do not graft that alternative onto this rear walking pose.

### Check before accepting the next image

1. Ignore heads initially: back, shoulders, hips and feet must agree on where
   Mama is walking.
2. Trace the near shoulder through exactly one near elbow to one free hand.
3. Locate the far shoulder and explain its occlusion; no arm may appear to
   originate from the near shoulder a second time.
4. The baby must occupy the far/front side of Mama, not float through her
   quills. His hidden body must fit behind the visible silhouette.
5. Head/eye contact with PinPin must not require an extreme neck twist.

## Round-window continuity: actual visible differences

The circular hub, radial spokes and rim define one fixed object. Reverse views
mirror the spoke layout horizontally; perspective changes angles and apparent
lengths, not the number or connectivity of spokes.

| Image | Observed radial layout | Continuity implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `arrival-01.png`, outside | Six spokes: top vertical, upper-right diagonal, right horizontal, lower-right diagonal, lower-left diagonal, upper-left diagonal. Broad uninterrupted left pane; no bottom vertical spoke. | Treat as the existing exterior reference if preserving this shot. |
| `arrival-02-v2.png`, inside | Seven spokes: top vertical, both upper diagonals, right horizontal, both lower diagonals and a bottom vertical. Broad uninterrupted left pane remains on the left. | Not the reverse of 01: adds a bottom spoke and leaves the broad pane on the wrong side. |
| `arrival-03-v2.png`, inside | Six spokes: both upper diagonals, left and right horizontal, both lower diagonals. Neither top nor bottom vertical. | Does not match 02 and is not the mirrored six-spoke layout of 01. Glass improvement alone did not solve frame topology. |

If retaining exterior 01 as the anchor, the **interior** frame must have top
vertical, upper-left, left horizontal, lower-left, lower-right and upper-right
spokes, with the broad uninterrupted pane on the **right** and no bottom
vertical. Use the same physical frame for 02 and 03, merely changing camera
position. Preserve glazing and characters while correcting actual wood;
do not simulate missing muntins with reflection streaks, or mirror the whole
scene and thereby reverse the house/characters.

This is a shot-to-shot structural comparison, not a claim that six spokes are
the only plausible window design. A different chosen design requires applying
it consistently to every view of this same window.

## Wider-scene check: select eight spokes instead

Further direct inspection of the published wider scenes changes the recommended
anchor. The six-spoke-preservation option above documents the first closeup
comparison only; **use a regular eight-spoke design for the repair**.

- Published `elder-r6-family-008.webp`, interior right window: eight visible
  spokes, with all four cardinal directions and all four diagonals present.
- Draft `arrival-05.png` and `arrival-10.png`, interior right window: the same
  regular eight-spoke arrangement. Some lower framing in 10 is partially
  occluded, but the visible layout agrees with 05 and the wider family scene.
- Published `elder-r6-family-003.webp`: exterior right window clearly has eight.
  Exterior left window has the same visible radial pattern, though Scooby
  obscures part of its bottom, so an independent count of every lower spoke
  is not possible in that one window.
- Published `elder-r6-family-002.webp`: this earlier exterior closeup already
  has the six-spoke irregularity seen in arrival-01. The drift was inherited
  from that reference; it is not sufficient evidence to override the wider
  architecture.

Standardize closeups 01, 02 and 03 to spokes at nominal clock positions 12,
1:30, 3, 4:30, 6, 7:30, 9 and 10:30 around a circular glazed hub. Account for
perspective without deleting spokes. Each spoke is solid continuous wood from
hub to rim; glass occupies the eight outer sectors and the center. Retain
subtle reflections. This symmetric design remains identical under horizontal
reversal, fitting both sides of the same window.

If family-002 remains selected in the expanded reader, its **review copy** also
needs this frame correction; otherwise the original closeup would still
contradict the newly consistent views. Preserve the original production image
and do not silently replace the official release.
