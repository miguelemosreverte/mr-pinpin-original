## Acceptance criteria

1. **Shared image language:** candidate and Elder reference must plausibly belong to the same animated book at normal reading size. Retain dimensional light and rich texture, but organize coat/quills into soft designed groups; avoid photographic strand noise and hard specular surface detail.
2. **Papa eyes and face:** visible warm iris and pupil separation; readable soft lids with enough light surrounding the eyes that they do not collapse to black buttons. No dark mask-like sockets. Rounded cheeks, friendly broad cream muzzle, soft transitions rather than deep facial contours.
3. **Papa identity:** visibly adult, robust father distinct from the Elder and child; retain brown/cream hedgehog identity and mature proportions. Do not solve eye warmth by making him a baby or copying the Elder's glasses.
4. **Scooby identity:** retain brindled brown/black coat placement, tan brows/cheeks/legs, pale muzzle, white chest bib, folded ears, friendly long-enough snout and curled tail. Stylization must not turn him into another breed or erase the user's face reference.
5. **Scooby rendering:** softer designed muzzle and brow planes, expressive amber eyes, grouped short coat, gentle nose highlights. Naturalistic photographic skin/fur shading should not dominate him.
6. **Attention:** gaze must have an identifiable story target. In an interaction study, eyes and head should visibly connect with the partner. In an arrival, attention should support the arrival beat rather than unrelated off-frame staring. Do not require mathematically impossible eye contact while preserving the rider pose.
7. **Scene integrity:** preserve plausible rider support, dog's grounded four-leg stance, correct limb counts, home facade and dawn geography. A style pass does not waive anatomy or continuity defects.

These are qualitative review gates, not claims of exact geometrical or stylistic identity. Full-scene viewing matters: an iris visible only in a crop is insufficient if Papa's face still reads as a dark mask in the chapter.
