# Elder revision 02 — English story draft

Status: complete review proposal; all selected images rendered and creator-reviewed. Short visual beats are intentional; image count is coverage, not a quota.

## 01. Path to the Elder

The day after he helped the lake, PinPin went to see the oldest hedgehog on Burrow Hill.

## 02. Leaf arch

What would a very old, very wise hedgehog be like?

## 03. The root home

There he was, beside a house tucked under the roots of a great tree.

## 04. A greeting without turning

“Hello, PinPin,” said the Elder, without turning. “I felt you would come today.”

## 05. How did you know?

“You knew? How?”

## 06. Leaves carry news

“The wind and the leaves bring news,” said the Elder. “I listen.”

## 07. The real question

“My golden quill helped the lake,” said PinPin. “Where did its magic come from?” “That is a good question. Come with me.”

## 08. A small thing needs help

On the way to the door, PinPin stopped. “That little plant is stuck.”

## 09. Lift carefully

He lifted the stick, carefully, so the leaves could unfold.

## 10. A place to grow

“There. Room to grow,” said PinPin. The Elder smiled.

## 11. Through the roots

“Now come and see where I keep my questions,” said the Elder.

## 12. A lived-in home

Inside were books, rolled maps, a soft bed and a little table stained with ink.

## 13. The Elder writes

“Do you write books?” “Sometimes. Today I was writing down what I noticed.”

## 14. Reading together

“Do you know everything?” asked PinPin. “Oh, no,” said the Elder. “That is why I read.”

## 15. Things worth keeping

A smooth stone. An empty shell. A seed in a little box. “Small things can make us ask big questions,” said the Elder.

## 16. Where everyone lives

PinPin found the lake on a map. All around it were little homes. “So many neighbours!”

## 17. The inner door

“There are older pictures downstairs,” said the Elder. “Would you like to see?” “Yes!”

## 18. Door opens onto a landing

The wooden door opened. Warm lamps shone down a stone stair.

## 19. First steps down

“Slowly,” said the Elder. “One step, then the next.” PinPin followed.

## 20. A safe turn

At the turn, the Elder waited. “All right?” “All right,” said PinPin.

## 21. Light below

Another little stair. Then the passage opened wide.

## 22. The plateau cave

They stood on a broad stone floor inside the hill. Great painted pictures curved around them.

## 23. Where the light comes from

“Sunlight!” said PinPin. Far above, a crack in the hillside let the day come in. Wall lamps warmed the darker corners.

## 24. Old sacred pictures

“These are sacred stories,” said the Elder. “Stories some forest families treasure.”

## 25. What does sacred mean?

“Sacred?” “Very precious to them. Something they treat with special care.”

## 26. What is religion?

“They believe in gods and spirits,” said the Elder. “Their stories, prayers and ways of giving thanks are part of their religion. Other families may believe different things.”

## 27. The Mother of Life

PinPin looked at a woman with a round belly. “Is there a baby inside?” “Yes. She is the Mother of Life in these stories.”

## 28. New life

“Is she a goddess?” “Yes. A goddess of fertility. That means new life: babies being born, and seeds growing into food.”

## 29. Baby and grain

PinPin found the painted baby. Then he found the tiny green shoots. “Both need looking after,” he said.

## 30. Prayer and thanks

“Some families bring seeds here,” said the Elder. “They pray. They ask the goddess for help, or say thank you.”

## 31. Care continues outside the story

“And then?” asked PinPin. “Then they go home and care for their babies and their gardens.”

## 32. The Shield Keeper

On the next wall stood a strong man with a great shield. Behind him, families huddled together.

## 33. A god of war

“Who is he?” “The Shield Keeper. In these stories, he is a god of war.”

## 34. What is war?

“What is war?” “When large groups of people fight one another. It can hurt people and destroy their homes. Here, he stands in the way of harm.”

## 35. What the shield protects

“If the darkness swept everything away, where would the little ones live?” asked PinPin. He looked at the tiny homes behind the shield.

## 36. The child is safe here

“This is a picture, and you are safe here,” said the Elder. “It tells us why protecting others matters.”

## 37. Strong enough to protect

“Does he like fighting?” “The best defender wants everyone safe. Being strong is not a reason to hurt someone.”

## 38. Light and darkness

Another picture showed light pushing back a great cold darkness. A little shelter glowed between them.

## 39. Is night bad?

“But I like night,” said PinPin. “Mama tucks me in.”

## 40. Restful night and harmful darkness

“So do I,” said the Elder. “Night gives us rest. The darkness in this story means danger that could take their home away.”

## 41. Many helpers

On the last wall were many small helpers. Some carried wood. Some brought food. Some held babies.

## 42. One shield is not enough

“Can the Shield Keeper do all those jobs?” “No,” said the Elder. “No one can.”

## 43. A community keeps life safe

“A strong arm can hold a shield. Other paws grow food and build warm homes. They need one another.”

## 44. What can I do?

“And my quill?” asked PinPin. “Your gift can help. First, look. Listen. Ask who needs you. You do not have to help all alone.”

## 45. A question inside

PinPin was quiet. He thought of the lake, the little plant, and the homes on the map.

## 46. Back to the stair

“Shall we go back?” asked the Elder. PinPin nodded. He had more questions now.

## 47. First steps up

Up they went. One broad step, then the next.

## 48. The familiar landing

There was the little lamp again. PinPin turned the corner.

## 49. Through the open door

The books and the ink-stained table came back into view. They were in the Elder’s home again.

## 50. The question goes on paper

The Elder closed the stair door. “What shall I write?” “How can my magic help?” said PinPin.

## 51. Old magic, more to learn

“Your quill carries old magic from our ancestors,” said the Elder. “There is still much to learn. We will take our time.”

## 52. Outside again

Outside, the leaves glowed gold. The little plant stood free beside the fern.

## 53. Tomorrow’s hilltop

“Tomorrow at dawn,” said the Elder, “meet me by the oak on the hilltop. We will begin with listening.”

## 54. A proper goodbye

“At dawn,” said PinPin. “Thank you.” The Elder smiled. “Bring your questions.”

## 55. The path away

PinPin set off. He did not know everything yet. But now he knew where to begin.
